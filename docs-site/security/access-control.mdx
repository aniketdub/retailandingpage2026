## Authentication

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Google sign-in" icon="google">
    Identity verified by Google, then matched to an existing staff record. **No self-registration** — an unrecognised Google account is rejected, not enrolled.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Email and password" icon="key">
    Rate-limited per IP address to blunt credential stuffing.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Sessions are signed tokens with an **8-hour idle lifetime**, refreshed on activity.

## Continuous authorisation

A session is not a permanent grant. Role and clinic are re-read from the staff record periodically rather than trusted from the token for its full lifetime.

<Warning>
  Consequences worth knowing:

  - A **deactivated** staff member loses access within minutes, not at token expiry
  - A **role change** takes effect within minutes without forcing a sign-out
  - A **missing staff record grants nothing.** The failure mode is closed.
</Warning>

## Module-level authorisation

Every dashboard screen checks access **on the server as its first action**. Navigating directly to a module URL you lack access to returns nothing — the gating is not client-side hiding.

The set of guarded routes is verified structurally: a test suite sweeps every API route and every page for its authentication guard and clinic scoping, so **adding a route that forgets either one fails the build**.

## Tenant scoping

Clinic identity comes from the authenticated session, never from user input. See [Architecture](/security/architecture#tenant-isolation).

## Rate limiting

Applied to the interfaces where abuse would be expensive or damaging:

| Interface | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Sign-in | Credential stuffing |
| AI generation | Cost and abuse |
| WhatsApp send | Number reputation |
| Inbound WhatsApp, per phone | A malfunctioning or hostile sender |
| Voice calls | Runaway outbound loops |

<Note>
  Rate limiting is per running instance rather than globally coordinated. The effective ceiling scales with the number of instances. It is abuse protection, not a hard billing cap — stated because a vague claim here would be misleading.
</Note>

## Audit logging

Logged with staff identity, timestamp, and the affected record:

- Sign-ins and failed sign-in attempts
- Permission and role changes
- Staff activation and deactivation
- Patient record creation, edit, and deletion
- Consultation and prescription creation and amendment
- Invoice issue, amendment, refund, and cancellation
- Discounts granted
- Inventory adjustments and wastage, with reasons
- Data exports, including full-clinic exports
- Photo exports and collection additions

Audit entries are retained independently of the records they describe. **Deleting a patient does not delete the record that the patient was deleted**, or by whom.

## Support access

RetentionOS staff hold no standing access to your clinic's data after handover. Support access is requested, time-boxed, and logged.

<Tip>
  Ask for that log any time. If a vendor cannot tell you exactly when their own staff accessed your patient data, that is the answer to a different and more important question.
</Tip>

## What you should do

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Review staff access monthly" icon="user-group">
    Anyone left? Deactivate them. Anyone changed role? Update it. Five minutes at month-end.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use Google sign-in" icon="google">
    It removes the shared-password failure mode, which is the most common real-world breach path in small clinics.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do not share accounts" icon="user-slash">
    A shared front-desk login makes the audit trail useless. Named accounts cost nothing.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Restrict billing deliberately" icon="lock">
    Fewer people with refund and discount permission is a materially lower fraud surface.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
